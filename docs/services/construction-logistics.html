Construction logistics planning estimates and manages the site setup, temporary works, and material delivery requirements that support the main construction programme. Logistics costs – including tower cranes, hoists, scaffolding, welfare facilities, site roads, and material storage – are frequently under-estimated in tenders. BIM Takeoff models logistics requirements from BIM data, capturing phased site setup, crane reach analysis, and material delivery scheduling.

Why Site Logistics Separate Winners from Losers

Reactive Site Management

Most sites plan logistics as they go:

Crane conflicts discovered mid-lift

Material deliveries block site access

Inadequate laydown space causes delays

Trade stacking and sequence conflicts

Temporary works designed multiple times

Client surprises about site impact

The Logistics Cost Multiplier

Poor logistics planning adds to programme duration, and every lost week adds to prelim costs. Planning the site properly, before mobilisation, is cheaper than fixing conflicts once the crane is already on hire.

Our Proven 5-Step Logistics Planning Process

Step 1: Site Analysis & Constraints

Duration: 3-5 days

What We Do:

  • Site visit and survey (topography, access, boundaries)
  • Review site constraints (neighbours, highways, utilities)
  • Identify permit and approval requirements
  • Assess existing structures and obstructions
  • Understand client operational constraints
  • Review programme and key milestones

What You Receive: Site constraints report with opportunities/risks

Step 2: Concept Logistics Planning

Duration: 5-10 days

What We Do:

  • Develop site layout options (2-3 alternatives)
  • Preliminary crane/hoist positioning
  • Identify temporary works requirements
  • Plan major deliveries and access routes
  • Assess welfare and facility needs
  • Present options with pros/cons

What You Receive: Concept logistics plans for review

Step 3: Detailed Design & 3D Modelling

Duration: 10-15 days

What We Do:

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should we engage logistics planning on a project?

A: Ideally at RIBA Stage 3 (Spatial Coordination) or earlier for complex sites. This allows logistics constraints to inform design decisions. Absolute latest: 8-12 weeks before site mobilisation. Urban projects with crane permits require earlier engagement (16-20 weeks before start) to allow for authority approvals.

Q: Can you help us win tenders by showing better logistics solutions than competitors?

A: High-quality logistics planning in a tender submission demonstrates competence and de-risks the project for the client - clear site planning, phasing strategies, and 4D animations set a submission apart. Priced per package, quoted before we start. No hourly billing and no fee for scope we did not agree.

Q: Do you handle temporary works design or just coordination?

A: We coordinate temporary works requirements and prepare schedules, but detailed structural design (scaffolding, falsework, propping) must be by certified Temporary Works Design companies. We work closely with TWC (Temporary Works Coordinator) and TWS (Temporary Works Supervisor) to ensure integrated planning. We can recommend specialist TW design firms if needed.

Q: Can you optimise our existing site layout to improve efficiency?

A: Yes. Mid-project optimisation is common. We analyse current layouts, identify bottlenecks and inefficiencies, and propose improvements to material placement, routes, and double-handling. It works best when implemented at a natural break point, such as topping out or a phase transition.

Q: What if we don’t have a BIM model yet?

A: We can work from 2D drawings to create 3D site models for logistics planning. For early-stage projects, even sketch designs are sufficient for concept logistics. The earlier we’re engaged, the more value we add - but we can deliver useful planning at any stage, even with limited design information.

Q: How accurate are your crane coverage analyses?

A: Accuracy depends on the manufacturer data we base the analysis on - we use actual crane specifications from Liebherr, Potain, Wolff and others, including wind speed deratings, load charts, and boom configurations. Quantities are measured to the drawings and the specification you send. Where the information is incomplete we list the assumption rather than absorb it. Modelling coverage in 3D before crane selection catches gaps and over-sailing conflicts that guesswork misses.

Q: Can you create 4D animations for client presentations?

A: Yes. 4D animations are useful stakeholder communication tools - showing construction sequences, phasing, and logistics operations over time. They help with client confidence, planning approvals, neighbour engagement, and internal team coordination. Cost depends on the length and complexity of the animation and is quoted before we start.

Q: Do you provide on-site support during construction?

A: Yes, weekly or monthly site visits for logistics coordination support are available. Services include: reviewing actual vs planned layouts, updating plans for design changes, optimising phasing based on progress, coordinating major lifts and deliveries, and attending planning meetings. Priced per package, quoted before we start. No hourly billing and no fee for scope we did not agree.

Q: What’s the typical ROI on logistics planning?

A: The return comes from what planning avoids: crane hire errors, programme slippage, rework and material damage from a poorly sequenced site, and delays caused by missed regulatory approvals. We don’t quote a multiple, because it depends entirely on the project - but the cost of fixing a logistics conflict on site is reliably higher than the cost of catching it in the model first.
